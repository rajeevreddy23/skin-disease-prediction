# 5-Minute Final-Year Project Live Demonstration Script
## Project Title: Skin Disease Prediction Using Machine Learning with Live Image Capture and Web Dashboard

---

### Overview & Preparation
- **Total Presentation Duration**: 5 Minutes (300 seconds)
- **Presenter Role**: Senior ML Engineer / Project Lead
- **Setup Requirements**:
  1. Laptop with webcam connected.
  2. Terminal 1: Streamlit dashboard running (`streamlit run app/app.py`).
  3. Browser tab open at `http://localhost:8501`.
  4. Project report (`docs/project_report.docx`) and presentation slides (`docs/presentation.pptx`) ready on desktop.

---

### Minute-by-Minute Script

#### ⏱️ Minute 0:00 – 0:45 | Title, Problem Statement & Software Architecture
> **[Action]**: Stand before the panel with the DermaScan AI dashboard visible on screen. Introduce yourself and project title.

**Presenter Speaks:**  
"Respected panel members and external examiner, good morning.  
Today, I am proud to present our final-year engineering capstone project: **'Skin Disease Prediction Using Machine Learning with Live Image Capture and Web Dashboard'**.

Skin cancer—especially malignant melanoma—is one of the fastest-growing malignancies worldwide. If caught in its early localized stages, patient five-year survival is greater than 99%. However, outside of specialized oncology centers, access to experienced dermatologists is severely constrained.

To address this, we engineered an end-to-end, **software-only clinical decision support system**. Notice that our system requires **no expensive external microcontrollers or hardware rigs**; the entire pipeline—from live laptop camera capture, hair artifact removal, deep transfer learning inference, to explainable AI and persistent database logging—runs locally and securely on this single laptop."

---

#### ⏱️ Minute 0:45 – 1:30 | Dataset & Clinical Preprocessing (DullRazor & CLAHE)
> **[Action]**: Click on the **'Live Diagnosis & Grad-CAM'** tab. Point to the Preprocessing toggle in the sidebar.

**Presenter Speaks:**  
"Our models are trained on the gold-standard **HAM10000 benchmark dataset**, containing 10,015 histopathologically validated dermatoscopic images across seven categories: Melanocytic Nevi, Melanoma, Basal Cell Carcinoma, Benign Keratosis, Actinic Keratosis, Vascular Lesions, and Dermatofibroma.

In real-world dermatological imaging, dark hair strands and poor contrast often deceive standard convolutional networks. To solve this, we implemented the clinical **DullRazor algorithm**. As you can see in the sidebar:
1. DullRazor performs mathematical **Black Hat morphological filtering** to extract the dark linear hairs into a binary mask.
2. It then applies **Telea inpainting** to restore the underlying skin pigments without distorting the lesion border.
3. We also provide **CLAHE** (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) in LAB color space to enhance diagnostic texture."

---

#### ⏱️ Minute 1:30 – 2:30 | Live Diagnostic Demonstration & Top-3 Probabilities
> **[Action]**: Select 'Curated Sample Gallery' (or switch to 'Laptop Webcam Live Capture' and click Capture). Select **'Melanoma (mel)'**. Click **'Run Deep Learning Diagnostic & Explainability Analysis'**.

**Presenter Speaks:**  
"Now, let us perform a live test. The system offers three input modalities: live capture from our laptop webcam, high-resolution clinical photo upload, or benchmark test samples.

I select this suspicious pigmented lesion and trigger inference. Within just **35 milliseconds**, our deep learning engine delivers a comprehensive diagnostic card:
1. **Predicted Class**: `Melanoma (MEL)`.
2. **Confidence**: Flagged with high statistical confidence.
3. **Risk Level Badge**: Color-coded in red as **'Critical Risk (Malignant)'**.
4. **Top-3 Ranked Probabilities Chart**: Melanoma is ranked highest, followed by differential possibilities (Nevus and Benign Keratosis).
5. Below the diagnosis, the system displays an educational clinical overview detailing common ABCDE signs—Asymmetry, Border irregularity, and Color variation—along with urgent advice to consult a certified dermatologist for biopsy."

---

#### ⏱️ Minute 2:30 – 3:30 | Explainable AI via Grad-CAM Heatmaps
> **[Action]**: Scroll down to the **Grad-CAM Explainable AI Visualizer** panel. Adjust the **Heatmap Opacity slider** in the sidebar.

**Presenter Speaks:**  
"In medical applications, high accuracy alone is insufficient; clinicians cannot trust an opaque black box. Therefore, we integrated **Explainable AI using Grad-CAM** (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping).

Look closely at this side-by-side visualization:
- On the left is the raw input lesion.
- In the center is the normalized activation heatmap generated by calculating the gradients of the melanoma class score with respect to the final convolutional feature maps.
- On the right is the blended overlay.  
Notice that the deep red and yellow focus regions strictly align with the **irregular, hyperpigmented central blotch and scalloped border** of the lesion, completely ignoring normal healthy skin and peripheral noise. This proves that our network has learned genuine diagnostic dermatological pathology."

---

#### ⏱️ Minute 3:30 – 4:15 | Prediction History Audit & Simulated Edge Mode
> **[Action]**: Navigate to **Tab 2 ('Prediction History')** and then **Tab 4 ('Edge Device Architecture')**.

**Presenter Speaks:**  
"Moving to Tab 2: Every diagnosis performed is automatically persisted into a local **SQLite relational database**. Here, clinicians can search previous scans, filter by risk severity, and export complete audit logs via this **'Download History as CSV'** button with a single click.

Now, let us inspect Tab 4: Although our system is software-only, our architecture was designed for future IoT edge deployment. We built a **Simulated Device Mode** running on an asynchronous background daemon thread. It faithfully mimics the three-tier hardware flow:
1. An **ESP32-CAM sensor layer** capturing frames and simulating JPEG serialization.
2. A **Raspberry Pi edge gateway broker** validating packets and maintaining an asynchronous queue.
3. The **Laptop inference engine** processing incoming frames in real-time.  
Notice the live telemetry console tracking baud rates, simulated Wi-Fi RSSI, packet counts, and queue depth."

---

#### ⏱️ Minute 4:15 – 5:00 | Model Benchmarking, Future Work & Conclusion
> **[Action]**: Switch to **Tab 3 ('Model Analytics & Comparison')**. Show the comparative bar chart, Confusion Matrix, and ROC curves.

**Presenter Speaks:**  
"To scientifically validate our methodology, we trained and compared three distinct neural architectures on the hold-out test set under identical stratified conditions:
1. A **4-stage Custom CNN baseline**.
2. **MobileNetV2** with inverted residual bottlenecks and transfer learning.
3. **EfficientNetB0** with compound scaling.

As demonstrated in this comparative table and bar chart, **MobileNetV2** and **EfficientNetB0** achieved superior hold-out accuracy and weighted F1-scores, while maintaining sub-50ms CPU latency. Our multi-class ROC curves show high Area Under the Curve across malignant categories.

**Future Scope**:  
In the future, we plan to compile our best MobileNetV2 model into an 8-bit quantized TensorFlow Lite (TFLite) binary to execute natively on a physical Raspberry Pi 5 with a polarized optical dermatoscope attachment, and integrate multi-modal patient clinical records.

To conclude: We have built a complete, fully functioning, explainable, and software-ready dermatological decision support platform. Thank you, and I now welcome your questions."
